What Is Held-out?

Machine LearningModel Evaluation
August 22, 20263 min read

Held-out data is a portion of data deliberately kept outside a particular training or development decision so that we can test how a model performs on unseen data.

Suppose we want to train a spam classifier and have 10,000 labeled emails. We use 8,000 as the training set, 1,000 as the validation set, and the remaining 1,000 as the test set.

First, the model reads the 8,000 training emails repeatedly. After each prediction, it compares the result with the true “spam / legitimate email” label, calculates the error, and updates its internal parameters through gradient descent. This is direct learning of the model parameters.

What exactly do the 1,000 validation emails do?

They act like a practice exam during development. After training a candidate model, we ask it to predict these 1,000 emails that were not used for training, then use the results to decide which approach to take in the next experiment.

For example, we train three candidate configurations on the same 8,000 training emails and obtain the following validation results:

Comparing three candidate configurations on the validation set
CandidateMain settingTraining accuracyValidation accuracyFalse-positive rate on legitimate validation emails
ASmaller model91%89%2.4%
BLarger model99%86%4.8%
CLarger model + regularization96%92%1.7%

Looking only at the training results, B appears best at 99%. But it reaches only 86% on the validation set, suggesting that it may have memorized details of the training emails and performs worse on new ones. C does not have the highest training accuracy, but it performs best on validation, so we choose C.
